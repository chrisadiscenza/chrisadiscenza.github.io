#### <b> Let's Play a Game</b>

#####<b>Set Game<b>


Introduce the set game. If you have multiple games, be sure to keep them separated. You can mark the side of the stack if cards with different color marker. There should only be one of each card in the game. 
Explain that the cards have the folowing traits:
3 Shapes: Diamond, Oval, Squiggle (the students might have other fun names for this like wiggly or worm)
3 Colors: red, Green, Purple
3 Quantities: One, Two, Three
3 Fillings: Empty, Solid, Striped. 

How to play: 

Shuffle the deck. 

Then have a student put 12 or 15 cards face up in ann array. Array might be a new terms for the students but will help them organize their thoughts visually when it comes to multiplication.

The students are playing against eachother. They are looking for three cards, called a Set, that satisfy the following rule. If two cards share a trait then the third must also have that trait. In other words, if one card "feels left out" among it's friends then the three cards do not form a set. 

When a student thinks they see a Set, they should shout "Set!" (or say it quietly) and tap the three cards. Then everyone needs to agree and discuss if it is a set or if there is a card that "feels left out". 

Once a Set is found, the student keeps it and three cards are pulled from the stack to replace them in the array. 

If there are no Sets in the array of cards, three more may be added. 

Once the stack is depleted, the student with the most Sets is the winner. 


How many Red Squiggle cards are in the Set Game? Fixing the shape and the color only leaves the quantity and the filling to vary. Students might see this as 3 options x 3 options. Another way is to draw a diagram of 3 rows of red squiggles that change in quantity so that the columns change in filling. Then it becomes visually clear. 


 Now they should be able to determine the total number of cards in the Set Game? In other words, the card-inality of this card game. Puns aside this is the word used to describe the amount of objects in a collection. Since there are three options for each trait, the cardinality of the Set Game is $3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 81$


One nice fact about the game is that given any two cards, there is a unique third card that completes the Set. 

Have the students explain why three cards are a Set or why they aren't a Set. 


####<b>LCR Game<b>

Left Center Right is a simple straightforward game where each player starts with the same number of tokens and takes turns rolling dice that indicate what the player should do with their tokens. have the students choose a starting player in which that players has the respionsibility of recording the total number of rounds until the game ends. This data is important for comparisons with multiple games. Each player will initially roll three dice and distribute one token per die. Rolling an L indicates giving a token to the player on their left, rolling an R indicates giving a tokem to the right, and C indicates that they give a token to the center of the table. Finally, rolling a dot indicates that the player does kepps their token.  With students it is helpful to introduce the rule that any player reicieving token must say: "I appreciate you". This seemingly silly gesture helps to keep the atmosphere pleasant.

Have the students play a few games then disuss the worksheet questions and the following concepts:

-How many rounds did the LCR game last until somebody won? Could the game go in forever? What is it about the game that causes it to end? 
Describe the total amount of tokens they start with as the total initial wealth. f they each start with 3 tokens then the total wealth is the nummber of players $\times$ 3 tokens. Have them think about what roll of the dice descreases the total wealth versus what redistributes the the wealth. 
If they don't see it righ away, encourage them to notice that the C decreases the wealth (and never says "I appreciate you" when taking tokens). This game is an example of what's called a _dynamical system_ and the concept that the center plays is called a _Sink_. It's like a blackhole in a galaxy (which is also an example of a dynamical system). 

Have the students think of variations of the game that might include a roll of the dice that might counter act the C roll. This could be for example replacing one of the dots with a T that would indicate taking a token from the tin. have them discuss whether this variation would cause the game to go in forever with one or more players accruing infinite wealth. In a dynamical system this is an important concept called _Stability_. In the example of our solar system, stability determines whether the planets eventually spiral into the sun, or fly out into deep space, or continue orbiting forever. For the example of financial markets, stability determines whether the economy collapses into depression, accumulates into a monopoly, or continues to function for everyone. 

Left Center Right is a game that on the surface doesn't have any strategy but has some interesting results as many games are played and with more players. Computer simulations by Benjamin Richeson and David Richeson found that for small groups the last person from the starting position has a slightly favorable chance of winning while the starting player has a slighty less favorable chance. However as the number or players increase beyond 5, these positions slide to second to last and second respectively.
