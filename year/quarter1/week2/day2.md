## <b> Rules of Deduction</b>



<b> Definitions: </b>

Semantics: The interpretation of a statement as being true or false. If you tell the students that you are wearing a red shirt. They will immediately respond with that's true or false. Especially if it's false. They are applying semantics to your statement. It's separate from the statement itself. 

Syntax: The rules of deduction. These are discussed below. Notice that there is no mention of the statements being true or false in the context of syntax. It's all about what can be deduced from given statements and the connecting words: <b>and, or, implies, not</b>. 

Converse: If $B$ then $A$.  $B \implies A$ 

Have the students write an example sentence of this. Then have them discuss how it the converse is different from $A \implies B$. A cause implies effect example makes it obvious that they are different. 

Contrapositive: If not $B$ then not $A$. $\neg B \implies \neg A $ 

Again,  have them write an example sentence for this and compare it to $A \implies B$. Have them notice that the contrapositive is an equivalent statement to $A \implies B$. 


**Symbols: **

$A \implies B$: If $A$ then $B$

$A \land B$: $A$ and $B$

 $A \lor B$: $A$ or $B$  
 
 $\neg A$: not $A$



Discuss the rules of deduction: 

Power Rule:
$A, A \implies B \vdash B$

And Rule:
$A \land B \vdash A, B$

Or Rule: 
$\neg A, A \lor B \vdash B$

Suppose a restaurant menu says: Dessert: Pie or Cake.
But there is a sign on the wall that says they are out of pie. 
Use the Or rule to deduce what you are having for dessert.
